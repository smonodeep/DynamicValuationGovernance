# Four-Question Research Programme

## RQ1 — Market Formation
**How are residential property prices formed in a data-constrained market?**

Focus: HPI dynamics, rental fundamentals, CPI/inflation, unemployment, monetary conditions, city heterogeneity, relative price position/momentum, and distributional effects such as Quantile Regression.

## RQ2 — Valuation Uncertainty
**What market and data characteristics generate uncertainty in automated residential property valuation?**

Potential mechanisms: transaction sparsity, property heterogeneity, illiquidity, geographic fragmentation, limited comparable transactions, changing market regimes, and rental/price information gaps.

## RQ3 — Performance Observability
**How are AVM performance and valuation uncertainty measured and disclosed across markets?**

Potential evidence: Mean/Median Error, MAE, RMSE, PPE10/PPE20, hit rates, geographic/property-type/segment performance, confidence measures, error distributions, monitoring frequency, and model limitations/disclosures.

### Core construct
**Valuation Uncertainty Observability**

Underlying valuation uncertainty is not the same as observability of valuation uncertainty.

## RQ4 — Governance Transmission
**How should institutions translate observable—or poorly observable—valuation uncertainty into credit-risk controls?**

Transmission chain:

`AVM → Valuation Uncertainty → Governance → LTV / LGD / PD / ECL`

Potential mechanisms include valuation eligibility thresholds, review triggers, confidence thresholds, LTV haircuts, exception handling, human review, monitoring, and model limitations/disclosures.

### Incremental-value question

Does a valuation-governance signal provide incremental information beyond the existing credit-risk framework?

Conceptually:

\[
Y = f(S_{baseline}, S_{governance})
\]

with:

\[
H_0: \gamma_{governance}=0
\]

versus:

\[
H_1: \gamma_{governance}\neq0
\]

## Central Concept

A potentially distinctive construct emerging from this programme is:

**Uncertainty about Valuation Uncertainty**

An institution may face substantial valuation uncertainty while having limited ability to observe, quantify, or characterize it. This creates a governance problem distinct from simply having an inaccurate AVM.

## Research Programme Positioning

- RQ1 establishes the housing-market empirical foundation.
- RQ2 explains the sources of valuation uncertainty.
- RQ3 studies whether uncertainty is observable through AVM performance measurement and disclosure.
- RQ4 examines how institutions should transmit valuation uncertainty into governance and downstream credit-risk decisions.

The U.S.–India AVM comparison is primarily relevant to RQ3; the AVM-to-credit-risk governance framework is primarily relevant to RQ4.

Do not assume cross-country differences in AVM disclosure or performance. Establish them empirically through a systematic provider-level disclosure and performance dataset before building theory around the asymmetry.
